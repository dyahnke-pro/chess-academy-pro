## Move grading and verdict wording (grades, cost words, the verdict store)
The fault band itself is shared: win% lost at 5/10/20, in accuracyService.gradeMove/bandForWinPctLost. Everything around it is split. There are two label vocabularies (MoveClassification and moveRating.MoveQuality, plus a third MoveQuality in tacticTypes). Mate rules exist in three places: gradeMove, classifyCpLoss and classifyMoveFull. A single Learn move is graded up to four times from different engine reads (gradePlayedMove, detectSlip, backwardLook, inaccuracyCall). There are also five side graders: tacticClassifier, classifyEvalSwing (handed to the LLM), liveCoachTriggers, a dead gradeGuess, and an engine-free GuessTheMove grader.

The verdict store is the right shape for G7 but is half-wired. Only Learn and Review write to it, and only batch analysis reads it. It keeps faults only. One plausible path stores a walk-into-mate as 'fine', which then overrides Review's blunder.

The rulebook asks for Miss, Best, Excellent and the "only move or turns the game" Great; none of these is computed. The 'walked-into-tactic' and 'missed-forcing-win' reasons are dead because their inputs are hard-coded to 0.

The wording is copied about seven times (three verdict ladders in groundedAnswer, QUALITY_CLAUSE, CLASSIFICATION_LINES, costWords vs cpToWords, evalPhrase). It speaks numbers (V8), talks about "the engine" and "I" (V1), and drops the better move's squares in Review and Learn (S10).

For the one brain: grading should be ONE computer producing ONE stored record per move, holding grade, reason class, cost-kind, outcome-band crossing, better move and refutation line, with their squares. That record would fill the TEMPTING-CHOICE and THE-LINE roles and feed the student record. Each surface's speaking floor would move into the one decider.

## Why-a-move / better-move reasons (explainBestMoveGrounded, deliberation, moveInsight, betterMoveReason, compareTwoMoves, computeMoveWhy and siblings)
This area answers one chess question: "why is this move good, and why is the other one worse?" About 20 separate computers answer it, and they never share a structured fact. Six answer "why the better move is better": explainBestMoveGrounded, betterMoveReason, deliberation.moveWhy, compareTwoMoves, explainMoveOrder and assembleCompareMovesAnswer. About nine answer "what this move does": computeMoveWhyDetail, groundedMoveWhy, quietPurposePhrase, describeMoveMerit, strategicWhyLed, studentMovePoint/quietMovePoint, buildReviewMoveTeaching and assembleMovePurpose. Five pick the "tempting choice": deliberation alternatives, buildRejectedTempting, tacticalRead.pickTempting, refutedAlternative and assembleAlternativesAnswer. Three explain move order: moveOrder.ts, checksFirst and explainMoveOrder.

Review alone can attach four different reasons to one flagged move, and Learn, Play and chat each use a different head computer. This breaks F2, D1 and D7.

For the one thinking chain (F0c/F18) this area should be REASON, TEMPTING CHOICE + why it fails, and THE LINE. deliberation already has that shape: it lists the candidates, rules each out with a proof, then gives the verdict with its reason and line. But it is turned off in the opening, it weighs only 3 candidates, and its clause reaches positionFacts as text alone, with no squares or lines.

Most reasoners return a bare string. That includes explainBestMoveGrounded (about 15 call sites), betterMoveReason (which throws away the square whyBetter computed), moveWhy, groundedMoveWhy and studentMovePoint. So arrows have to be re-read from the prose (S10).

Fix direction: one typed reason fact {role, kind, squares, lineUci, stakes} that every surface reads. compareTwoMoves' ablation method should become the engine for "why", with the missing kinds added (king safety, structure, activity, prevention).

## Turning points, criticality, importance, thesis/recap producers
This area is the brain's "is this moment worth stopping for, and what was the game about" layer. It is fragmented. There are five separate gap computers (scanCriticality, readCriticalMoment, speedRunReads.choiceGap, positionCharacter.sharpGap, criticality.ts gap12) and five decided-game gates that disagree (600cp, WDL 900, 75 win-%, 300cp, a raw material count). Four turning-point pickers run on Review: selectTurningPoints (student, win-%), turningPointCandidates (either side, cp), buildCriticalMomentQuestion (fan count), and the narrative recap (the first three mistakes in game order). On top of them sit three more key-moment definitions (the review "critical" flag at |swing|≥150, KeyMomentNav, statusBandChange). Review speaks about "the moment" through 8 producers with no shared order or say-once.

The strongest computers for the one chain are:
- readCriticalMoment as TEMPTING CHOICE + why it fails, because its discard proofs come from each line's own engine line.
- selectTurningPoints as HABIT, because it counts the cause across the game.
- computeImportance as the gate. It decides when a moment speaks, so it fills no role itself.
- selectTeaching as GOAL/thesis. But its thesis cannot fire in phase narration (no evals are passed), and Review runs it twice with different inputs.

Dead or dropped:
- onlyMoveSequence (no callers).
- the computeCriticality score (no reader).
- the turning-point question chips (built, never asked).

Almost nothing here carries moves or squares. Proofs, the "why", what the move allowed, the hinge and the thesis are all prose, so S10 arrows on the lines these computers describe are impossible today. The fix: each fact returns its line in UCI along with its text.

## Threats: detection, prevention, answers (detectNewThreat, computeMustDefend, threatAnswer, latentDanger, danger lanes)

## Tactics / pins / forks / hanging pieces detectors and the tactic vocabularies
This area is the coach's tactical vision, and it is wide but split. One static scanner, tacticsDetector.detectTactics, covers about ten motifs and reaches 34 files. Beside it sit a move-based classifier with weaker gates (tacticClassifier), two definitions of 'hanging', six pin tests, six fork tests, three discovered-attack tests (one of them called 'x-ray') and two skewer tests that disagree. Several 'is it real' gates sit on top (isRealPin, isScenicPawnPin, verifyForkOnBoard, tacticWinsMaterial). So the same board can be taught as a tactic on one surface and filed as something else in the record. That breaks the shared vocabulary the loop depends on.

Concrete defects: computeMustDefend misses a defended piece attacked by a cheaper one. A removal-of-the-guard hole is drilled with defensive-move puzzles. Live lists are cut to 5 and missed tactics to 10. Rating decides how far ahead the coach looks and when it speaks. Several computers are half-wired: forkTrick only says a trick was sidestepped, latentDanger only looks from the student's seat, and pinPressureThreat and threatCheck have no callers.

For the one thinking chain, these computers supply the OBSTACLE (their threat), the GOAL (your tactic), REMOVE-IT (threatAnswer), the TEMPTING CHOICE and THE LINE (tacticalRead, pinPressure) and KNOWLEDGE (the TACTIC_INVARIANT text). But most of their outputs carry squares without the move that executes the tactic, so arrows have to be guessed back out of the sentence.

The biggest missing computer is the first gem's lesson, 'a pin breaks when the pinned piece moves with check'. Nothing computes it, and forkTrick deliberately skips checks.

## Plans and structure: deriveNextPlans, structurePlan, planRace, planArc, namedPawnStructure, mastersPlanRead, pawn-structure reads
This area has many computers but no ONE plan computer. The 'what's the plan' question is answered by about seven producers on different surfaces: structurePlan (Learn/Play through positionFacts, Review thesis, chat), deriveNextPlans (Review, chat, Learn's learnMoveTeaching), namedPawnStructure (Learn structure lane, chat), mastersPlanRead (Learn), the reviewStrategicOrientation beats (Review), planChoice and planArc (Learn only), and positionalRead (Learn quiet plies). They overlap. The passer and isolani plans are each said by 3-4 producers in different words, and dedupe relies on ideaKey strings. The wing, passer and near-promotion definitions disagree between files.

For the one thinking chain (F0c/F18), these computers naturally fill GOAL (structurePlan / deriveNextPlans / mastersPlanRead), OBSTACLE (weak pawns, worst piece, colour complex), REMOVE-IT (breaks, blockade, reroute), TEMPTING CHOICE (planChoice, planRace's 'you can't just race') and LINE (lookaheadPlan / planArc). None of them is composed into that chain today: each speaks its own sentence.

Two structural defects block S10 and the loop:
- (1) The plan producers return prose without the squares and moves they name. deriveNextPlans, structurePlan, planRaceClause, namedPawnStructure and planChoice all drop the squares, so plan talk is not arrowed.

The fix direction:
- One plan fact type that carries kind, side, squares and line.
- One structure inventory (describeStructure) with one wing and passer rule.
- Plan computers emit to the decider instead of each surface.
- Add the missing nuggets: the rule of the square in races, the chain base, two weaknesses, prophylaxis.
