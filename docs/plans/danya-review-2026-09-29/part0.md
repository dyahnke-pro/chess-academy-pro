## 2. Arc across a video

- **Game first, then a second pass.** 34 of 50 videos jump back to an earlier ply after the game (120 backward jumps in total). The second pass holds the theory tour, the "what if they had…" lines, and a moral. 175 beats carry no move at all: these are pure commentary or flashbacks.
- **The typical sequence:**
  1. Name the opening and place it by popularity (FREQ).
  2. Declare a one-sentence plan early.
  3. In the middlegame, name the targets and weaknesses ("two clear plans").
  4. Convert by simplifying or cashing in.
  5. Recap and give a moral, usually with a theory detour.
- **One story line.** Moves are tied back to the declared plan. -Am8glebGwQ explains every move as serving f4-f5; 26ZXZEiudhA says "the plan carried from opening to endgame"; 1PI3xfMiUE4 names a trick at [14] and pays it off at [33].
- **Callbacks.** Earlier ideas come back ~2–4 times per video: "that's why the pawn went to c4" (-FRuDd), "Remember this h5-h4 idea" (4GIsh7cTsHc).
- **Density is bimodal.**
  - About 60% of beats carry one idea or are bare move-glosses. Some videos are almost entirely glosses (4_Ev1a1_2Mg is 100% "You play X").
  - 29 monologue beats run over 600 characters, across 16 videos. They cluster at critical moments or in the recap and pack in 5–15 ideas.
  - So attention is spent unevenly on purpose; this is TRIAGE applied to the narration itself.

## 3. Computable from engine + board, or needs authored content

**Computable:**
- REFRAME: diff the plan inputs before and after a move.
- COUNT: attacker and defender counts.
- TEST: null-move and multi-move probes.
- TIMING: engine best-move changes when a resource disappears.
- SIMPLIFY: eval margin plus trade-availability.
- RETREAT-IS-FINE: the best move is backward and the eval holds.
- DEFENSIVE-PIECE-VALUE: the piece's defence map.
- KING-HUNT: escape-square count; a quiet move beats the checks in the PV.
- TRADE-ADVANTAGE: material given versus change in eval, plus a structural delta.
- STUDENT-MISCONCEPTION: a population-frequency move that the engine refutes (explorer + engine).
- TRIAGE: gapCp is small, so the choice doesn't matter.

**Partly computable:**
- OPPONENT-READ: needs clock data and move timing.

**Needs authored content:**
- ANECDOTE / EXEMPLAR.
- MNEMONIC / STUDY-METHOD (a template could cover "remember the unusual move").
- SELF-ERROR: only honestly possible when narrating a real game against engine truth. A generated version would be fake modesty; do not synthesize it.

## 4. How this refines the 22

- **More common than expected:**
  - PLAN appears in essentially every video that has a middlegame.
  - ALOUD (calculation including rejected moves) is in ~35 videos and dominates the long beats.
  - COST (trade-off) shows up nearly every time he pushes a pawn.
  - FREQ / theory placement is in 32 videos.
  - RATING is said aloud in ~20.
- **Less common than expected:**
  - ELICIT is rare as a real question-and-wait. It's ~10 rhetorical "why?" lines, which he always answers in the next breath.
  - FEAR is ~8 videos.
  - PROPHY as a named concept is ~10.
- **Refinements:**
  - COND (when a pattern doesn't apply) usually comes as a rule stated and then its exception: "never f6 — except with tempo" (-9fPYlSEkJ0 [16]), "pushing pawns in front of your king doesn't automatically get you mated" (3XUh57mV8a8 [22]). A RULE→EXCEPTION pair is the real unit.
  - MOMENT and ALOUD merge in practice: the critical moment is where the monologue happens.
  - He withholds answers only briefly and rhetorically. He almost always names the move, together with its reason. That supports the new "Learn names the move with its reason" rule.
