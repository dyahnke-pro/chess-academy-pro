**2. Video-level arc**

- **Sequence.** He names the opening, gives his recommendation and why that move order. Then the game is played live. Then he rewinds to ply 3–10 for theory, alternatives, history and the engine check. Then he closes with takeaways.
- **The rewind pass is the norm:** about 27 of the 39 substantive videos (the 48 minus those nine) replay from an earlier ply after the game ends.
- **Explicit takeaway summary:** about 12 videos, for example 6si_sctYAS0 [7], 8L-c [5] and B7r1bgPEyIQ [23].
- **One story per game.** He sets up the student's plan with ORDER and PLAN, finds the weakness it creates, attacks it, converts, and in review names the single moment it turned on. In 6nEI2p3ftgk that is c6 making d6 backward; in CfCoA7jQu84, g5 creating the f5 hole.
- **Density is bimodal.** About 40% of beats are one bare clause, mostly the opponent's routine moves. Critical plies carry 2–4 ideas. Seven or more videos contain mega-beats that pack a whole middlegame into one entry (898k4qkY0vg [16], 91lZ8sfu-XU [10], 9JUlD51s6zE [30], C6vYlx-lS6g [20], A-HTpnOfVcE [29], B7r1bgPEyIQ [21]).

**4. Refinements to the 22**

- **ORDER and RULE are the most frequent structures by far.** Almost every substantive video has them, e.g. Di-5aO2lddU [19] "parrot a repertoire one move out of order and you're lost".
- **RULE usually comes paired with its exception, in about 8 videos.** Examples: 6nEI2p3ftgk [42] "deliberately bend the rule"; CfCoA7jQu84 [23] "a knight on the rim with a future", and at [25] recapturing on an outpost with a pawn; DqBi8Zt412s [15] the king to d1. So COND should be modelled as RULE plus EXCEPTION, not as a separate structure.
- **ELICIT is often a timed puzzle, not only a question.** B7r1bgPEyIQ [22] asks the viewer to take 3–5 minutes; 8QxDmN_BMaU [18] is "White to play and win".
- **EVALHON uses spoken numbers,** such as "+0.8 versus +1.5" and "plus four".
- **RATING is common, in about 8 videos,** with explicit numbers: 1300–1400, 1600, 1700–1800, 2000, 2300.
- **FEAR is common, in about 9 videos,** and is usually phrased as an illusion of a threat: "don't fear ghosts", "there's no magic in chess" (A-HTpnOfVcE [17]), "only an illusion of one" (ADPy7sCbiUU [9]).
- **PROPHY is refined:** "prophylaxis only when necessary; you can prepare an answer instead of preventing the move" (CZC9BI_joNU [15], CpaJYTaDHM0 [16]).
