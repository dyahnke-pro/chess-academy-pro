# The mistake causes and their lessons — for David's sign-off

There is one list of causes, kept in the error computer (`MistakeCauseKind`). Every card points its lessons at it. Each cause has one recipe: LESSON (a general rule, with this board inside it) → CONSEQUENCE → BETTER MOVE + ITS JOB (only once that move is past) → HABIT (only when your record on that skill is red or grey). Learn speaks after the move only, and never names the move you just played or your next move.

`{…}` is filled from the board. Each stem has a second phrasing; the two are rotated, never chosen at random.

## C. Lesson rules for the 9 motifs that have none today

These are taught on both seats. "Theirs" is the version for the opponent's mistake, said after your reply.

|---|---|---|---|
| hanging piece | A piece nothing guards can just be taken. | When their piece is loose, take it before anything else. | Every move, check what is loose — theirs and yours. |
| deflection | A defender that is dragged away stops defending: the {piece} on {sq} was guarding {target}. | Pull the guard away and what it guarded falls. | Ask which of their pieces is doing a job, and how to drag it off. |

## D. One habit table

Every habit sentence comes from one table, keyed on the record's skill tag. The words are fixed on purpose: repetition builds the habit. The record decides how much is said:
- RED or GREY: the full sentence;
- AMBER: one clause;
- GREEN: nothing.

This replaces the per-computer habit lines: knee-jerk, empty check, keep pressing, count before you take, and the fundamentals' "Next time".
