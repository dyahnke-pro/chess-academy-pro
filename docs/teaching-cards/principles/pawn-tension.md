# Keep the tension (often)

## Principle
Taking first often releases the pressure in the opponent's favour — it can open a line or develop their piece; keeping the tension keeps them tied down. Release it when the exchange gains something concrete.

## When it applies / exceptions
Disputed/conditional: sources frame it as a choice, not a rule ('the side that takes first often helps the other develop' is a tendency).

## Deterministic check
- Tension = mutually attacking pawns; the student's capture recaptured by a developing move (piece leaves the back rank) or opening a file the opponent's rook reaches first → the lesson.

## Phrasings
1. Keep the tension; taking first can help them develop.
2. Your capture brought their piece into play.
3. Don't release the tension unless the trade wins something.
4. Holding the tension keeps their pieces tied down.
5. Wait for them to take; then you choose the recapture.
