# Cramped or passive position — free it

## Principles
1. **Trade pieces.** Fewer pieces need less room.
2. **Make a freeing pawn break** — strike at their pawn chain's base, the way out of a cramp.
3. **Don't make more passive moves;** each one lets them add pressure.
4. **Mirror (they are cramped): avoid trades** and keep them squeezed.
- Disagreement: Gserper (chess.com) warns that chasing trades misses better moves; trade only pieces that are in each other's way or that are their best attackers.

## Deterministic check
- Cramped: student total legal moves ≤ 0.7 × opponent's, AND ≥2 student pieces with ≤2 legal moves, AND the student has fewer squares controlled in the opponent's half than they have in the student's (space count: squares attacked beyond the 4th rank from each side).
- P1: an even trade (SEE = 0) of one of the low-mobility pieces, or of the opponent's most active piece (highest mobility).
- P2: a legal pawn push/capture that contacts the base of an enemy pawn chain (the rearmost pawn of a diagonal chain) or a central enemy pawn, with SEE ≥ 0.
- P4: same test, seats swapped → advise keeping pieces on.

## Phrasings
1. You're short of space, so trade a piece and breathe.
2. Strike at the base of their pawn chain to free your game.
3. A cramped side needs one pawn break; find it before they tighten.
4. Swap the pieces that trip over each other.
5. They're short of room: keep the pieces on and let them suffocate.
