## Who / why
Eric Rosen (IM, chess.com `imrosen`) is THE Stafford Gambit popularizer —
his video "The Trappiest Opening in Chess?" turned it into an online
phenomenon. He plays the whole 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 complex from Black; in
his corpus the Stafford-accepted + Four-Knights decline are his most-played
e5 lines. He has said he has played this structure "close to a thousand
times" online.

## Core teaching principles (his framing)
1. **Develop fast, before White consolidates the extra pawn.** Black is
   down a pawn after 4...dxc6 — the whole point is speed and pressure, not
   material.
2. **Pressure f2.** f2 is the point of attack in almost every line — the
   Bc5 + Ng4 + Qh4 battery aims straight at it.
3. **Use the queen aggressively** — ...Qh4 (mating ideas + h-file) or
   ...Qf6/...Qd4.
4. **Don't fear sacrifices.** Many ideas are knight or bishop sacks
   (...Nxe4!, ...Bxf2+, ...Nxg3) — the initiative is the compensation.
5. **Keep the initiative.** "The moment you start defending, your
   compensation disappears." Attack, attack, attack — no slow maneuvering.
6. **The recapture 4...dxc6 is correct** (not 4...bxc6): it opens the
   d-file for the queen and the c8–h3 diagonal for the light bishop, and
   keeps a healthy 3-vs-2 majority feel with fast development. Both bishops
   come out fast; ...Bc5, ...Bg4/...Bf5, ...Qd-something, ...O-O-O.

## His signature plan in the accepted main line (5.d3 Bc5)
- ...Bc5 (eyes f2) → ...h5 (the bait / h-file lever) → ...Ng4 (hits f2/h2)
  → ...Qh4 (mating battery + h-file). This ...h5/...Ng4/...Qh4 motif is the
  heart of his Stafford. He deliberately plays ...h5 "to set psychological
  and tactical bait" rather than the engine's top move, then storms the
  h-file with ...h4.
- Famous demonstration: **Rosen beat GM Sergey Erenburg (2555) in 14 moves
  at the 2024 FIDE World Blitz Championship**, finishing 14...Qh1# — the
  Ng4/Qh4/h-file motif in its purest form against a strong GM.
  Source: https://www.attackingchess.com/how-eric-rosen-used-the-stafford-gambit-to-beat-a-grandmaster-in-14-moves/

## The legendary trap (5.d3 Bc5 6.Bg5??)
6.Bg5 looks like a normal pin of the f6-knight to the queen. It loses on
the spot to **6...Nxe4!!** — a queen sacrifice:
- 7.Bxd8 Bxf2+ 8.Ke2 Bg4# — a Légal's-Mate pattern: the f2-bishop covers
  the king's flight squares while Bg4 delivers mate. White wins the queen
  and gets mated anyway.
- 7.dxe4 Bxf2+ 8.Ke2 Bg4+ wins the white queen.
- 7.Be3 Bxe3 8.fxe3 Qh4+ then ...Nxg3 leaves Black crushing.
This is "the trap that built the Stafford's reputation."

## Other trap motifs seen in his corpus
- **...Bxf2+! Kxf2 Nxe4+** forks (after a careless White King-walk).
- **...Ng4 → ...Nxe3/...Nxh2** wrecking ideas when White plays Be3 without
  prophylaxis.
- Whenever White plays a slow developing move (Bc4, d3, Nc3) without h3,
  the ...Ng4 jump is on.

## How White correctly neutralizes it (so we teach honest limits)
- **6.Be2** (dodges ...Ng4 hitting a piece, prepares O-O) is the main move.
- **h3** at the right moment is "essential — it prevents all ...Ng4 ideas
  before they start." 5.Nc3 + h3 is one of the most practical anti-Stafford
  setups.
- The "fortress" plan: c3 + Nd2 + d4, build a center, make Black's bishop
  "bite on granite," consolidate the extra pawn. This is why the Stafford is
  objectively a GAMBIT (dubious with best play) — we teach it as an honest,
  initiative-for-a-pawn sharp weapon, not an equalizer.
- **4.Nf3** (retreating the knight): after 4...Nxe4 5.Nc3 Nxc3 6.dxc3 the
  game is a quiet, equal Petroff — White's safest decline; Black is simply
  fine and fully developed.

## The decline: 3.Nc3 Four Knights (his BEST scorer in the corpus)
White sidesteps the gambit with 3.Nc3 (no Nxe5). Black answers **3...Bc5!**
(the Italian Four Knights / "Stafford mood" — bishop straight to c5 eyeing
f2) rather than the symmetric 3...Nc6. Play continues 4.Bc4 (or 4.Nxe5) with
...Nc6/...d6, ...d6, ...h6, and the same f2/kingside pressure if White ever
drifts. This is a fully sound, fighting line — his highest win-rate e5 tab.

## The 3.d4 push
3.d4 Nxe4! (not 3...exd4) 4.Bd3 Nc6 — Black gives the pawn back / keeps the
knight active, fast development, equal-to-better. He scores well here.
