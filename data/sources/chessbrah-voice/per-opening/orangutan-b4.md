## What the series is

A low-to-mid-band offbeat repertoire. It is the least theoretically serious of his
opening series and the most trap-driven, which makes it a poor masterclass
candidate and a *good* source of punish-gem candidates.

## The recurring trap he wins games with

He describes it as happening over and over: Black develops the queen's knight to
c6, White pushes b5 attacking it, Black's knight goes to b4, and the knight has no
route home and is lost. He calls out the pattern explicitly — Black always plays
...Nc6, White always plays b5, the knight always goes to b4, and White wins a
piece by around move five.

🚨 **This is a lead, not a trap.** Per the hand-curation doctrine (locked
2026-06-01), it needs: the explorer frequency for the ...Nc6 and ...Nb4 moves at
club ratings, chess.js legality and orientation, Stockfish verification of the
refutation graded at the quiet end of a best-play playout, and a theory
cross-check. Only then does it get a tier. The caption-level description is
plausibly the well-known Sokolsky trapped-knight motif, but "plausibly the
well-known motif" is exactly what G3 forbids authoring from.

## The setup rules

- **Bb2 and keep the long diagonal open.** The b2-bishop is the opening's reason
  for existing; several of his choices are about not blocking it.
- **e3 to develop; c4 and Nc3 in that order** — the same c-pawn-before-the-knight
  rule he states in the Colle series, phrased here as "never Nc3 in front of the
  pawn."
- **The b4-pawn does positional work**: he notes it takes c5 away from Black's
  bishop, so a natural developing move for Black simply isn't available.
- **Qc1-b2 battery** when the bishop has been traded or needs support, generating
  pressure down the long diagonal.
- **a3 to hold b4** when Black challenges it, rather than pushing on.
- **Nd5 as the standard jumping square** once c4 and Nc3 are in.
- **Queen-trap motifs**: he repeatedly punishes early Black queen sorties (…Qg5
  and similar) with tempo moves — Nf3 and Nd4/Nc3 hitting the queen, in one case
  trapping it outright after ...Nc6.
