## The frame (his taxonomy of Black's 3rd moves after exd5 Qxd5 Nc3)

- **…Qa5** — the most popular retreat (tree: 247g). His treatment: d4, Bd2,
  Bc4, Nf3 development (tree continuation `d4 Nf6 Bd2 c6 Bc4 Bf5 Nf3`).
- **…Qd6** — the "Tiviakov" line: sharp, was fashionable, now considered
  dubious — but dangerous if White is inaccurate (tree: 97g @ 71.1%,
  `d4 Nf6 Nf3 c6 Bc4 Bg4 h3`). Won't be faced until ~1800-1900.
- **…Qd8** — the underrated retreat people wrongly dismiss; not easy to prove
  a big edge against if Black follows correctly (his SPINE: `d4 Nf6 Nf3 e6
  Bc4 Be7 O-O O-O Qe2 a6` — quiet edge, develop and press).
- Against ALL queen moves: start with d4, grab the centre.
- Black's correct paradox (his teaching): not racing development but …c6 to
  blunt the d4-d5 advance — which sets up the punish below when skipped.

## The …Nc6 punish gem (the "huge mistake" he calls out)

After exd5 Qxd5 Nc3 Qa5 d4, the natural developing **…Nc6?** walks into
**d5!** — NOT Bb5 (met by …Bd7). The knight must crawl back to b8 or hop to
the "awful" e5 where it's a target. White develops with tempo: Bb5+ checks,
Bf4 + Nb5 hitting c7 — Black may collapse before castling. (Engine-verify the
exact sequence + evals before authoring; same red-target shape as the
anti-Pirc d5 punch — this is HIS signature space-punch, now confirmed as a
cross-opening THEME: Pirc …Nc6→d5, Belgrade …trades→exd5 hits c6, Scandi
…Nc6→d5.)
