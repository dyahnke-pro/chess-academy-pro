## Core philosophy (from Gordima distillation)

Three repertoire principles:
1. Lines lead to attacking or messy positions favoring tactical prowess
2. The king remains relatively safe despite complexity
3. Openings offer clear patterns without excessive strategic variation

## White repertoire (1.e4)

### Against 1...e5: Fianchetto system (g3)
Creating kingside attacking chances. NOT the Italian or Ruy — Naroditsky
plays a King's Indian Attack-style system against e5 in blitz.
Match: his actual chess.com data shows heavy KIA usage as white.

### Against French Defense: 3.Nd2 (Tarrasch)
Straightforward plans like Qe2-Qe3 after center closure. Minimal memorization.

### Against Caro-Kann: Fantasy Variation (3.f3)
Produces "wild" positions. He scores 75% in his Fantasy games — highest
of any of his Caro variations. The "wild positions" framing IS his voice.

### Against Sicilian: Bb5 lines + Alapin
"Combination of Bb5 lines with Alapin, notably featuring Bb5+ followed
by Bc4." So Rossolimo / Moscow Bb5+ mixed with the c3 Alapin system.

### Sidelines
- vs Scandinavian: Bd2 setup
- vs Pirc: h4 advance

## Black repertoire

### Najdorf Sicilian
Three sub-recommendations based on White's setup:

**vs Bg5 (English Attack starting move):**
"e6-Be7-Nd7-Qc7 concept" — minimizes memorization demands.
This matches my current pro-naroditsky-najdorf build EXACTLY:
spine is `Bg5 e6 f4 Be7 Qf3 Qc7 O-O-O Nbd7`.

**vs English Attack proper (6.Be3 or similar):**
**Unusual 5...a5** — creating "messy, tactical" positions.
"Engine absolutely hates it but Naroditsky wins almost all of his
games, probably because of a lack of knowledge from the opponents.
The positions are quite messy, with many tactical resources, even
if the evaluation jumps to +2."

**Standard approach (other 6th moves):**
6...e5 leading to kingside pawn storms.

### King's Indian Defense
Three sub-recommendations:

**Classical mainline (Be2 etc.):**
exd4 followed by f5 creating sharp tactical complications.

**Fianchetto Variation:**
Nd7-e5 with Qa5 targeting central pawns.

**h3 Systems (Makogonov etc.):**
Queenside pressure via Nc5-c6-a4-Qa5.

### Caro-Kann (as Black, against e4)
NOT explicitly named in the Gordima study (Gordima's covering his blitz
repertoire — Caro may be a less-common pick in blitz vs Najdorf/KID).
Per his actual game data: 3,546 Caro games, scoring 64%. Main spine is
the Advance Variation 3...c5 Botvinnik-Carls (528 games).

### vs 1.d4 (other than KID)
Per Gordima focus: KID is primary against d4. Per his game data:
- 4,432 KID games
- 2,830 Alekhine's Defense games (against e4 alternative)
- 1,475 Najdorf games

## Per-opening accent / Naroditsky catchphrases (compiled across sources)

From the Tartakower Caro variation article (TheChessLobster):
> Naroditsky highlighted that Black's natural plan involves placing the
> bishop on d6. He described this as "a very natural developing move,"
> **repeating this phrase three times** to emphasize its importance.
> Rather than viewing the doubled f-pawns as a weakness, Naroditsky
> framed the opening around the compensating advantages: ease of piece
> development and pressure on White's position. He demonstrated
> **9...Bg4!** as a critical attacking resource that makes "White
> already uncomfortable." The thematic rook sacrifice **Rxe3!** appears
> as a key tactical motif.

From the general speedrun principles (Listudy article — full content in
`naroditsky-teaching-principles.md`):
- "A hook is a pawn next to your opponent's king" — attacking lever
- "The wishlist method" — work backwards from desired position
- "Flank attack → centre counter"
- "Space and time = money" — convert advantage or it evaporates
- "Kinetic and potential energy" — locked pieces have stored power
- "Successful attacks need pieces AND weaknesses"
- "Plans longer than 3 moves usually aren't"
- "Remove the cause, not the symptom" of tactical threats
- "Use your passive pieces" — best pieces stay where they are
- "The least valuable piece performs the task" (defensive duties)

From Naroditsky direct (speedrun catchphrases):
- "the bishop on d6 is a very natural developing move" (×3 in one Tartakower game)
- "White is already uncomfortable" (after pin moves like Bg4 in Tartakower)
